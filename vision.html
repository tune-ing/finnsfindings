October 14th, 2025

Use your resources

I finally reached out to my grandmother’s friend’s contacts in Denmark. It’s two months through this four month program, and while feeling down I was reflecting on my resources and remembered that this too is one. I also followed up with my college friend’s Danish friends. We will see if they get back to me, but I had a good chat with my grandmother’s friend’s friend who was an old man.

I want to claim loneliness in Study Abroad. But it cannot be denied that I am lonely by my own design. I thought I was being efficient when I scheduled my classes back to back, but what I didn’t realize is that the time that one moves between classes is a very valuable time to see “fringe” friends: people who you see at the same time and place consistently. I see people very rushed moving between buildings, but don’t really see the same people walking everyday which is strange.

I do run into people I know though, but it’s not consistent or ever expected. No one else’s schedule is memorized. I remember in ninth grade I was able to deduce an acquaintance’s schedule simply by when I saw her in the hallways. And we were all taking eight classes back then. Creepy, sure, but this shows off my point. Don’t worry, I don’t actively try to do this anymore. But even back at college, I can expect to see the same people in the dining halls pretty consistently. Sometimes there would be surprises, like an alum and their friend, but that would be rare and would line up with the class times.

So when I do see people I know, it’s always a pleasant and welcomed surprise. This happened three times today. Once with a friend from Wellesley who is also studying here. We weren’t super close back home, but that is one thing I’m grateful for are the new Wellesley friends I will have when we return. So we chatted for a brief moment before heading different directions to class. I then saw two others from my courses whom I exchanged waves and smiles with. That was so nice! An exchange doesn’t have to be a lot to make someone feel all warm inside.

I was surprised that I experienced it today as well as I would say my class this morning is a shitty time. Or at least until our past two classes. I was convinced that no one in our semester-long group project cared about school, and the professor wasn’t even engaged or had a pedagogy behind his lack of accessibility for learning (not sharing his slides). What changed in these past two classes? We went outside and I stood next to non tablemates. Literally. I talked to other people and realized that I was in a weird bubble of people who didn’t care. And it wasn’t that these people cared about this class either, but they did care about learning something and not just partying, or should I say drinking, their study abroad away.

Also, I talked to a new person in my Anthropology of Food class and it was a good reminder that there are people I like in this program. Even if the majority of the people I see are sorority, white, blonde, little-black-top-jeans-and-little-black-bag people doesn’t mean (1) that their personality is shallow and (2) that’s all there is. And yes, I want closer friends from study abroad, and yes I feel like I’ve missed a critical moment to make them easily. But I’m realizing now that the time isn’t over yet and to cherish it because I still can.

Today we talked about how farmers live in cycle with their crops, but hunters the time to kill is now. I am going to approach this second half (wow two months in and two to go?!?) like a hunter for friendship. I want to experience the high of friendship, that I got in small doses from my small chats and smiles and waves, in bigger qualities and for longer times.

Using your resources for friendships means that I will reach out to the Wellesley kids and try to spend time with them. Hang out with people multiple times a week. Get those reps in however they happen. Spend another hour with someone after class. Homework can wait for friendships to build. Continue to reach out to people. Be weird, but don’t be creepy. Jump ships. Pivot fast. Fail faster.

I feel like I’ve spent two months living alone with other people. Now is the time to draw on everyone and everything I know to create the high of life that I want to live in.
