Remember the boring days

I’m realizing that I should write about the boring everyday life things that I do. I know it will be nostalgic even if not so filled with everyday joy in a few months when I’m settled back at Wellesley. So here’s to a day in my life with commentary and descriptions.

November 5th, 2025

Well so much for consistency in writing and I’m currently not having a “boring” routine since I’m on my skipping path across Europe: Amsterdam, Oxford, Bologna. I’m currently in Oxford, staying with my friend Kimi from college, and exploring. This morning I played frisbee with the team here. It was fun and a beginner level so I felt that I really had to hype it up because it’s always cool when people join frisbee and as an outsider I had to show them that it continues even when not at your home institution.

But for my daily life in Copenhagen. I’ll include when my courses are, but those don’t dictate when I wake up, or rather get out of bed. Since the sun has been rising a lot later in the day, it’s hard to get out of bed before 8 even though I know that I work better in the mornings. Earlier in the semester, I had been going to the gym to cross train for frisbee but I haven’t been doing that recently. I will try to go back and do Woody’s workouts when I return and tournament season is over.

Oh yeah! My team won Mixed Indoor Danish Developmental nationals last weekend! That was an awesome feeling and we won spirit #frisbeenerds. I scored a handful of goals and had a good number of assists as well. And especially coming from the previous weekend where my team had lost all of our games, this was a good feeling that it wasn’t us (Mickie and I were on the same team at the GURLS hat tournament). I’m looking forward to this next weekend in Bologna playing on a women’s team.

Let’s take Monday. I will wake up around 8 and then scroll on my phone for 15 minutes to see if there are any worthwhile updates that I should know about: texts, headlines, abroad updates, etc. Then I will get up and brush my teeth and go to the bathroom. After that I head to the kitchen to make breakfast. Typically some toast with butter and instant coffee and maybe some blueberries. Or no toast, but skyr (scandinavian yogurt). After that I will check Canvas to see what readings I should do before class at 11:40 and do some of them. Around 11/11:20, I will bike over to N7 where Travel Writing is, and either sit in a random empty classroom to do some work or walk into class and chat with my peers until the period starts.

Class ends at 1, and my next class in ten minutes away on the third floor of Vestergade 23, the main building of DIS Copenhagen, so as soon as class ends, I look at the time (as sometimes it ends early), and decide if I should get a banana for lunch from 711 or Netto. By 1:15, I’m in Science Fiction in Literature, Films, and Videogames and I’m quite enjoying the class and the people are surprisingly great. Every time I leave that class at 2:25, I always have the urge to read a different essay reflecting on the human experience. That’s one thing I wish I had brought with me traveling: my kindle and downloads of all the recommended essays.

I keep thinking about how I want to get back into reading and how I want to just know more about the world. So I’ve started making a list of things, and I plan to download the wikipedia app and read through it everyday to get a surface level understanding of many things. Recently, I watched a Hank Green video on how we’re not addicted to our phones, but instead getting information.

After class ends, I will typically stick around in the Student Hub (V23) as I know myself and that I will get more work done if I stay. Additionally, I really enjoy seeing acquaintances, and I am more likely to see them there where everyone is passing through. Around 4 I will bike/walk back home. The best part of living in Solvgade is definitely the location as with a 10 minute walk is access to all of the metro and train lines through Marmorkirken, Norreport, and Osterport. By bike (with no wrong turns, it’s about 7 minutes from DIS) and by foot 20 minutes.

On Mondays, I will do some grocery shopping for the week and I’ve really been getting into canned fish so I’ll buy that, plus some greens, some snacks, perhaps some chicken if I’m not feeling fish, and skyr and berries, depending on what I know I have and want to make. DIS gives us a grocery card for $600 over the full semester, and I now feel the pressure of using all of it before the semester ends so I don’t mind splurging a bit on ingredients. Additionally, my meals stay pretty simple and do not have many steps to them.

The kitchen that I share with six other floormates is a full kitchen with a dishwasher, four burners, an oven, a microwave, two fridges, and a lot of plates, pots, silverware, cooking utensils, and mugs. I bought a rice cooker early on in the semester and it’s great as I can throw all of my ingredients in and then leave for around an hour and return and the rice and everything else will be cooked.

I will typically eat dinner in my room and then return to clean my rice pot and my dishes and put them into the dishwasher. I don’t really like our kitchen as the burners take a long time to cook the basics (30 min to boil water, over ten minutes to properly cook chicken all the way through and even then it’s iffy), so cooking normally takes longer than I am willing to budget for it which is why I like the rice pot as I can be productive in other ways than just attending it.

During dinner, I have been watching long form youtube videos, typically Hank Green or something educational. After dinner and cleaning my dishes, I will do some work (homework, stressing over Wellesley logistics, writing here, etc.) and catching up with people over WhatsApp. Then somehow my time is filled until 10 when I shower and then do my evening routine and go to bed.

That is a pretty standard day, and of course each day there is something new and different, but I’m no longer really tracking because I know my mental and physical routes to places without having to check my phone or question my assumptions because they are right. There’s a lot of comfort in the boring, but as I am currently exploring a different kind of routine, I’m enjoying feeling everything as if it was new again.
